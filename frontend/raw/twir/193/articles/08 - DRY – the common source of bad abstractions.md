# Item 8: DRY – the common source of bad abstractions

Source: [https://swizec.com/blog/dry-the-common-source-of-bad-abstractions/](https://swizec.com/blog/dry-the-common-source-of-bad-abstractions/)

Summary:
This article critiques the over-application of the DRY (Don't Repeat Yourself) principle, showing how premature abstraction can lead to harder-to-maintain code. Using a navigation menu example, it demonstrates how attempts to DRY up repetitive code can result in convoluted factories and configuration objects that obscure intent and hinder future changes. The author advocates for waiting until patterns are clear before abstracting and for favoring simple, composable components over rigid abstractions.

Key takeaways:
- Overzealous DRY can produce complex, hard-to-read abstractions that are difficult to adapt.
- Premature abstraction often fails to anticipate future divergence in requirements.
- Composable components are usually preferable to factories or config-driven abstractions.
- Wait for clear patterns to emerge before abstracting shared logic.
